# Behavioral / Culture-Fit

Recurring almost verbatim across Glassdoor-derived summaries, InterviewQuery, Exponent, and DataInterview. 🟢 Not an Amazon-style named "Leadership Principles" system — Example Company's stated culture values are **Respect the Individual, Act with Integrity, Serve Our Customers and Members, Strive for Excellence**, and behavioral interviewers reportedly probe ownership, customer obsession, and bias for action framed around these. 🟡

---

### Recurring questions

- "Tell me about a time you influenced a business decision with data."
- "How do you explain a technical concept to a non-technical stakeholder?"
- "Describe a time you worked with messy or incomplete data."
- "How do you think Example Company can help you advance your career?"
- "What are some learnings from the most challenging project you've worked on?"
- "How do you think your experience can help the company?"
- "Why do you want to join Example Company?" / "Why are you a good fit for this team?"
- "Tell me about an accomplishment you're proud of."

### How to prepare answers

Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) — but the sources are specific about one thing that separates a good answer from a mediocre one: **quantify the business outcome**, not just the technical work. The one concrete example surfaced in research (from a single-source account, so treat the exact phrasing as illustrative rather than a template to copy) — "improved search relevance by 12%, drove 3% conversion lift" — is the *shape* of answer to aim for: technical action → measurable business result, stated plainly.

Given this team's domain, have 2-3 stories ready that map naturally onto:
1. **A time you caught a data-quality issue before it caused a downstream problem** — directly relevant to a catalog-quality team (ties to the dedup/data-quality reasoning in [02](02_SQL.md) and [06](06_Duplicate_Product_Detection.md)).
2. **A time you had to make a precision/recall or false-positive/false-negative trade-off call, and how you decided** — directly relevant to fraud/compliance work ([04](04_ML_System_Design_Fraud_Detection.md), [07](07_Content_Compliance_Moderation.md)).
3. **A time you influenced a non-ML stakeholder (product, legal, ops) with a technical finding** — matches "explain to non-technical stakeholders" and "influenced a business decision" directly.
4. **A time a project didn't go as planned and what you learned** — matches "challenging project" and Example Company's "Strive for Excellence" framing (growth-oriented, not blame-oriented).

### What This Round Tests

- Whether your technical work translates into a business-outcome narrative a non-technical hiring manager can follow and remember
- Genuine familiarity with the kind of trade-offs this specific team makes daily (precision/recall, data quality, cross-functional influence) rather than generic "I'm a team player" answers
- Self-awareness and growth orientation, not just a highlight reel
